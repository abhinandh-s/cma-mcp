### Question 1

Case Scenario: [MCQs (i) to (iv) are based on this Case Study and the rest are independent MCQs]

Jupiter Manufacturing Ltd. (JML) is an unlisted public company with a paid-up share capital of ₹40 Crores, free reserves of
₹80 Crores, and a turnover of ₹450 Crores during the preceding financial year. The company's net profit for the immediately
preceding financial year was ₹6 Crores. During the current financial year 2025-26, JML faced severe market headwinds and
expects an inadequacy of current profits. Consequently, the Board of Directors proposes to declare a dividend out of
accumulated profits transferred to free reserves.

Mr. Rohan, an existing director in JML, is also a director in Titan Traders Ltd. Titan Traders Ltd. has failed to file its financial
statements for the last three continuous financial years (2022-23, 2023-24, and 2024-25). Mr. Rohan was appointed as a
director in Titan Traders Ltd. over three years ago and is currently continuing in that capacity.

JML convened a Board Meeting on 10th August 2025 to discuss and approve a lucrative contract with NeoTech Solutions, a
firm in which four out of the five existing directors of JML are partners. All five directors of JML attended the meeting.

In the context of the above scenario, answer the following questions (i) to (iv):
**(i)** Based on the financial parameters provided, which of the following statutory obligations is JML required to fulfil?
- **(A)** JML is not required to constitute a CSR Committee as its turnover is less than ₹1,000 Crores.
- **(B)** JML must constitute a CSR Committee, but it is not required to appoint an Internal Auditor.
- **(C)** JML must appoint an Internal Auditor, but it is exempt from constituting a CSR Committee.
- **(D)** JML must both constitute a CSR Committee and appoint an Internal Auditor.
**(ii)** For declaring a dividend out of free reserves due to the inadequacy of profits, what is the maximum absolute amount
JML can draw from its accumulated profits?
- **(A)** ₹4 Crores
- **(B)** ₹8 Crores
- **(C)** ₹12 Crores
- **(D)** ₹20 Crores
**(iii)** What are the legal consequences for Mr. Rohan regarding his directorships under the Companies Act, 2013, due to
the filing default by Titan Traders Ltd.?
- **(A)** He vacates his office as a director in Titan Traders Ltd. but continues as a director in JML.
- **(B)** He vacates his office as a director in JML but continues as a director in Titan Traders Ltd.
- **(C)** He immediately vacates his office in both JML and Titan Traders Ltd.
- **(D)** He is given a grace period of 6 months to rectify the default before vacating any office.
**(iv)** What will be the quorum requirement when the specific contract with NeoTech Solutions is taken up for discussion
at the Board Meeting?
- **(A)** The quorum will be 2 directors, but since only 1 non-interested director is present, the meeting lacks quorum
for this agenda item.
- **(B)** The 4 interested directors can participate after disclosing their interest, so the quorum remains 5 directors.
- **(C)** The quorum will be 1/3rd of the total strength (i.e., 2 directors), and the presence of 1 non-interested director
makes the quorum valid.
- **(D)** Since the interested directors exceed 2/3rd of the total strength, the matter must be compulsorily decided in a
general meeting.
**(v)** As per Section 10A of the Companies Act, 2013, a company incorporated having a share capital shall not commence
any business or exercise any borrowing powers unless a specific declaration is filed by a director. What is the statutory
time limit for filing this declaration with the Registrar?
- **(A)** Within 30 days of the date of commencement of business
- **(B)** Within 90 days of the date of commencement of business
- **(C)** Within 180 days of the date of incorporation
- **(D)** Within 270 days of the date of incorporation
**(vi)** Under the provisions regarding the term of auditors in the Companies Act, 2013, certain companies are subject to
mandatory rotation. What is the mandatory 'cooling-off' period for an individual auditor who has completed his
maximum permissible term of 5 consecutive years in a company?
- **(A)** He shall not be eligible for re-appointment as auditor in the same company for 1 year from the completion of
his term.
- **(B)** He shall not be eligible for re-appointment as auditor in the same company for 3 years from the completion of
his term.
- **(C)** He is permanently ineligible for re-appointment as auditor in the same company.
- **(D)** He shall not be eligible for re-appointment as auditor in the same company for 5 years from the completion of
his term.
**(vii)** Under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, a resolution plan submitted by a resolution applicant must be
approved by the Committee of Creditors (CoC) before it is presented to the Adjudicating Authority. What is the
minimum voting share required for the CoC to approve the resolution plan?
- **(A)** 51% of the voting share of the financial creditors
- **(B)** 66% of the voting share of the financial creditors
- **(C)** 75% of the voting share of the financial creditors
- **(D)** 100% of the voting share of the financial creditors
**(viii)** As per the Companies (Corporate Social Responsibility Policy) Rules, 2014, read with Schedule VII of the Companies
Act, 2013, which of the following activities does NOT qualify as eligible CSR expenditure?
- **(A)** Contribution to the Prime Minister's National Relief Fund
- **(B)** Projects undertaken specifically for the exclusive benefit of the company's employees and their families
- **(C)** Promoting education, including special education and employment enhancing vocation skills
- **(D)** Slum area development
**(ix)** In the context of Sustainability Management and corporate reporting, the "Triple Bottom Line" (TBL) framework
evaluates a company's performance and impact based on which of the following three core dimensions?
- **(A)** Profit, Productivity, Performance
- **(B)** Planning, Production, Promotion
- **(C)** People, Planet, Profit
- **(D)** Principles, Policies, Practices
**(x)** Under the SEBI (Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers) Regulations, 2011 (SAST Regulations), an
acquirer (along with Persons Acting in Concert) who acquires shares or voting rights in a target company must make
a mandatory open offer to the public shareholders. What is the initial trigger threshold of voting rights that mandates
this open offer?
- **(A)** 15%
- **(B)** 25%
- **(C)** 26%
- **(D)** 51%
**(xi)** According to the SEBI (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2018, what is the mandatory
minimum promoter’s contribution required in an Initial Public Offer (IPO), which is subject to a strict lock-in period?
- **(A)** 10% of the post-issue capital
- **(B)** 20% of the post-issue capital

- **(D)** 51% of the post-issue capital
**(xii)** Under the Competition Act, 2002, any person or enterprise proposing to enter into a "combination" (merger,
amalgamation, or acquisition) that crosses the prescribed asset or turnover thresholds must give notice to the
Competition Commission of India (CCI). Such a combination shall not come into effect until the CCI passes an order
or until the expiry of a statutory maximum waiting period from the date of notice. What is this maximum waiting

- **(A)** 30 days

**(xiii)** As per the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority Act, 1999, the IRDAI is a statutory body constituted to

- **(A)** A Chairperson, not more than 3 whole-time members, and not more than 3 part-time members
- **(B)** A Chairperson, not more than 4 whole-time members, and not more than 4 part-time members
- **(C)** A Chairperson, not more than 5 whole-time members, and not more than 4 part-time members

**(xiv)** Under the Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development (MSMED) Act, 2006, a buyer must make payment to
an MSME supplier for goods or services on or before the date agreed upon in writing. However, to protect the financial
health of MSMEs, the Act stipulates that in no case shall this agreed period for payment exceed a specific number of
days from the day of acceptance or the day of deemed acceptance. What is this maximum statutory limit?
- **(A)** 15 days

**(xv)** Under Section 4 of the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002 (PMLA), what is the standard quantum of
punishment prescribed for any person who commits the offence of money laundering (excluding offences connected
to the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act)?
- **(A)** Rigorous imprisonment for a term not less than 1 year but which may extend to 5 years, and a fine
